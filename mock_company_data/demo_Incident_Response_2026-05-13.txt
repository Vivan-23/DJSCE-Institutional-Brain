Engineering Incident Review — May 13, 2026
Attendees: Sam Rivera, Priya Nair

Priya Nair: The runbook draft is up. We had a real SEV2 yesterday — the search service was slow. I wasn't sure when to escalate it to SEV1.
Sam Rivera: Good question. Escalate a SEV2 to SEV1 if it lasts more than 30 minutes without mitigation, or if it starts affecting checkout. Error rate above 5 percent on a core flow is an automatic SEV1.
Priya Nair: And if the on-call engineer doesn't respond?
Sam Rivera: If on-call doesn't acknowledge within 10 minutes, the incident commander escalates to the engineering lead — that's me — and then to Dana. Never let an unacknowledged SEV1 sit.
Priya Nair: Should we roll back or fix forward?
Sam Rivera: Default to rolling back to the last known-good deploy first, then investigate. Only fix forward if a rollback isn't possible, and even then a second engineer must review the change.
Priya Nair: I'll add the escalation timing and the rollback-first rule to the runbook by Wednesday.
Sam Rivera: Great. Remember the guardrail — no production change during an incident without a second set of eyes.
