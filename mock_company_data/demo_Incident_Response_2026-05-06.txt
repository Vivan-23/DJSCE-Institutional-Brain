Engineering Incident Review — May 6, 2026
Attendees: Sam Rivera, Priya Nair, Dana Lee

Sam Rivera: We need a clear runbook for production incidents. When an alert fires or a customer reports an outage, the first thing is to declare an incident in the #incidents channel and assign an incident commander.
Priya Nair: How do we decide severity?
Sam Rivera: If customers can't log in or check out, that's a SEV1 — all hands, page the on-call engineer immediately. If it's degraded but usable, that's a SEV2. Cosmetic or single-customer issues are SEV3.
Priya Nair: And who pages whom?
Sam Rivera: For SEV1, the incident commander pages the on-call engineer and notifies Dana within 15 minutes. For SEV2, on-call handles it during business hours. Never deploy a hotfix to production without a second engineer reviewing it, even during an incident.
Dana Lee: That review guardrail is non-negotiable. Also, no communication to customers goes out without my sign-off on SEV1s.
Sam Rivera: Once mitigated, the incident commander posts a status update every 30 minutes until resolved.
Priya Nair: What about after?
Sam Rivera: Every SEV1 and SEV2 gets a written postmortem within 48 hours — blameless, with a timeline and action items.
