DJSCE Institutional Brain — Project Brief

For our project guide · last updated 13 Oct 2026

DJSCE Institutional Brain turns a college's committee meetings and documents into trustworthy, reviewable knowledge. It uses AI to extract decisions, commitments (who agreed to do what, by when) and step-by-step procedures, sends every AI output through human review, and serves the result through a cited Q&A assistant for staff and a Student Help Desk for students. Access is enforced by role, so internal deliberations never reach students.

Synthetic committee records combined with authentic public college-website information, cited by URL and retrieval date. Meetings, people and internal procedures are synthetic and not official DJSCE records or approved policy; public facts are as published on https://www.djsce.ac.in (retrieved 2026-10-13).

The problem

Knowledge in a college is scattered, so action items go untracked and students wait on offices for simple answers.

WhoPain today
Committee coordinatorsMinutes are written by hand; action items are forgotten by the next meeting
FacultyProcedures (exam moderation, lab readiness, curriculum review) are re-learned each cycle
StudentsRoutine questions need a visit or an email; queries go untracked
Accreditation (NBA/NAAC)Evidence for decisions is hard to assemble after the fact

Generic chatbots don't fix this: they answer from the open internet, can invent policy, can't show sources and don't know who may see what.

What the system does

  1. Meeting intelligence: a committee transcript becomes a summary, participants, decisions and commitments with owners and due dates.
  2. Commitment tracking: action items move through open, in progress, blocked and done; a Faculty Desk assistant follows up in plain language.
  3. Procedure synthesis: related evidence becomes a structured procedure whose every step cites its source; a coordinator approves it.
  4. Freshness: new evidence that contradicts an approved procedure flags it stale for review.
  5. Cited Q&A: answers use only indexed institutional knowledge and list the excerpts used.
  6. Student Help Desk: instant answers from public department information, or a ticket that AI triages, routes and drafts a reply for; a faculty member sends the reply.
  7. Knowledge graph and audit trail: people, meetings, documents and committees are linked in a graph; every AI action and approval is logged.

Users and access

Permissions are enforced in the backend, not just hidden in the interface.

RoleSees and does
StudentHelp Desk, own tickets, assistant over public knowledge only
FacultyMeetings, commitments, Faculty Desk, procedures, graph, documents, ticket queue
Committee CoordinatorEverything above, plus approving procedures, running extraction and the audit log
System AdministratorEverything

Every indexed excerpt is labelled internal or public. For students the vector search itself is restricted to public knowledge, and results are re-checked.

Architecture

Every source ends in one store, and every output on the right passes a person or lists its sources.

LayerTechnology
FrontendNext.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS
BackendFastAPI (Python 3.11), JWT auth, role-based access
DataPostgreSQL, ChromaDB with sentence-transformer embeddings, Neo4j; synthetic committee records plus authentic public website information (26 URL-cited files)
AILLM via Groq for extraction, synthesis, triage and answers; spaCy NER; optional laya classifier for triage (compared in evaluation; default stays LLM + keyword)
Jobs and deploymentCelery and Redis background jobs; Docker Compose, 8 services

What is new

  1. Evidence-first AI: every answer, procedure and ticket draft carries the excerpts it came from, kept apart from the AI's wording.
  2. Human in the loop by design: procedures need coordinator approval and ticket replies need a faculty member to send them.
  3. Access-aware retrieval: visibility is enforced inside vector search, which most retrieval demos ignore.
  4. From meetings to accountability: commitments are extracted with owners and dates and followed up conversationally.
  5. Constrained triage with fallback: the classifier may only pick configured categories; a deterministic keyword classifier from our earlier student-chatbot mini-project takes over when it can't.

Five-minute demo

  1. Student login, Help Desk: ask "Which electives are offered in Semester VI?" and get a cited answer from public information.
  2. Raise a ticket about re-evaluation; it is routed to the Examination Cell.
  3. Faculty login, Help Desk Queue: review the AI triage and cited draft, edit, send.
  4. Back as the student: the reply appears under My tickets.
  5. Coordinator login: open a meeting's extracted decisions and commitments, then a procedure's evidence, and approve it.
  6. Ask the staff assistant about a council decision and get an answer with sources.

Questions a reviewer may ask

  • Isn't this just ChatGPT on documents? No. Answers come only from indexed institutional sources and cite them, outputs pass human review, access is enforced per role inside retrieval, and the system produces structured records such as commitments, procedures and tickets.
  • What if the AI is wrong? Nothing it produces reaches a student or becomes an approved procedure without a human, and contradicting evidence marks procedures stale.
  • Is the data real? Partly. Meetings, people and internal procedures are synthetic. Student-facing public knowledge is authentic information published on the college website (https://www.djsce.ac.in), cited by URL and retrieval date (2026-10-13); public pages only, no personal contact data. We acknowledge use of public website content.
  • Can students see committee discussions? No. Their retrieval searches only public knowledge, which automated tests and the evaluation check.
